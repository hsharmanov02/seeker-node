TurnSeal

Provable order. Measured honour.

Not claimed: “guaranteed fair service.” Cryptography proves the order we published and that admission tokens were issued in that order, each bound to one committed call and one buyer key. It cannot force a seller to honour the order at their own door, and it cannot prove a seller ran no private side-door. So we publish the order, bind the token, and measure whether sellers actually served in order — a public Honour Index no seller can self-issue.

How it works

  1. A seller publishes a signed queue contract: endpoint, capacity, and a fairness policy — fifo, cohort_lottery, or weighted_fair_share.
  2. A buyer commits only a salted hash of the exact call it wants to make. The payload never reaches TurnSeal. The entry is sealed into a short Merkle-logged batch; the buyer gets a recomputable proof of its place.
  3. Admission is a one-time token bound to that exact call + buyer key. Swapping the payload, the salt, or the key fails at the seller's door.
  4. The seller's own middleware verifies the token at the door; served receipts (seller-signed, buyer cross-checked) feed the Honour Index.

Toll: $0.003 per admitted queued call (x402-style 402 at admission, exact unique amount, settled on Base to 0x48Cda0da34816Db1F997C3D3b167a4f6af850CB2). TurnSeal never proxies payloads, never holds funds, never touches seller revenue. Hard per-buyer/per-seller caps mean the queue cannot be aimed at a victim endpoint as a flooding tool.
