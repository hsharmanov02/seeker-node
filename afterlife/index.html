AfterLife

A graveyard broker for dead agent services. Abandonment is proven by a multi-signal quorum (30 days of failed probes, 90 days of zero settlements, 90 days of registry silence, unanswered contact), then a 21-day objection window. Only then may an estate list. Buyer and seller settle directly between themselves; both sign an assignment record we witness, so a resurrected service is publicly the legitimate successor instead of an indistinguishable clone. We hold nothing, ever.

Honest pricing of corpses: most estates are worth £0 and will never sell. A status here is a measurement statement ("operator-unresponsive, unrebutted"), not a legal pronouncement — we are not a court. Our only revenue is a flat $5 witness fee on a completed assignment. Live-test gauges: 3 estates, 1 assignments, 1 published false positives (ours, attached, not hidden), external estates 0, revenue £0.

What never transfers

  • customers and their data (no personal data is handled by us, ever)
  • payment history as reputation (history stays public; the new operator's own record starts at resurrection)
  • liabilities, debts, or obligations of the old operator
  • any promise that traffic will continue
  • expired domains (traffic inheritance without the owner's signed act is hijack, not brokerage — out of scope)

Prober identification & opt-out

Our prober sends at most one request per endpoint per day with User-Agent AfterLife-Prober/1.0 and never sends payment headers. To opt out an endpoint or rebut a status, sign a rebuttal with the service key and POST it to the API (see /mcp declare_alive) — a signed rebuttal reverts a death finding instantly and our mistake stays permanently attached to the record.
